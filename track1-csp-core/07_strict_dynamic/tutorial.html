01 — The Big Analogy

Imagine a nightclub where the doorman checks ID for exactly one person: the event's official DJ, who has a verified, pre-approved wristband (the nonce). Once the DJ is inside, though, anyone the DJ personally walks in through the door — their own crew, brought in one at a time, in person, by the DJ themselves — gets in too, no separate ID check. But the venue's own regular staff list (the guest list at the front — the "'self'" allowlist)? Irrelevant tonight. Once this special "DJ's-personal-escort-only" policy is in effect, the normal guest list stops being consulted for anyone at all — even the venue's own bartenders, if they try to walk in through the front door without being personally walked in by the DJ, get turned away.

02 — What Happens, Step by Step

  1. Server sends script-src 'self' 'nonce-<fresh>' 'strict-dynamic'.
  2. A static <script src="/static-child.js">, written directly in the HTML with no nonce, is blocked — 'strict-dynamic' makes the browser stop consulting 'self' entirely for parser-inserted scripts.
  3. The nonced root <script> runs (Proof A) — same nonce-matching mechanism as Module 5.
  4. That root script calls document.createElement('script'), sets its .src, and appendChilds it into the document — no nonce attribute anywhere on this new element.
  5. That dynamically-inserted child runs anyway (Proof B) — because it was inserted, at runtime, by a script the browser already trusts, via a non-parser-inserted DOM API. Trust propagated.

04 — The Code, Explained

The propagation mechanism — public/index.html:

const child = document.createElement('script');
child.src = '/dynamic-child.js'; // NO nonce attribute set here at all
document.body.appendChild(child);

05 — Limits / What You Cannot Do Yet

✗ CAN'T DO YET

  • Rely on `'self'` for ANY script once `'strict-dynamic'` is in the policy — it's fully disabled, not just deprioritized
  • Propagate trust through `innerHTML`-inserted markup
  • Combine two entirely separate policy sources and reason about which wins (Module 8)
