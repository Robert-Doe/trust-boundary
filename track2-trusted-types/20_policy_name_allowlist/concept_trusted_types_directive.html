Vocabulary Web · The Trusted Types Directive FamilyConcept: trusted-types (directive)

This is the GUEST LIST. A CSP directive whose value is a space-separated list of exact policy NAMES (plus the special keywords 'allow-duplicates' and 'none') — it decides who's allowed to call createPolicy successfully at all, and under which names.

What it looks like

Content-Security-Policy: trusted-types dompurify dompurify-policy 'allow-duplicates'

Where you've seen it

  • Module 20 — introduced; a disallowed name throws Policy "..." disallowed, a reused name throws a DIFFERENT error, already exists
  • Module 24 — needed TWO names simultaneously (DOMPurify's own internal "dompurify", plus the course's own "dompurify-policy") — a real bug caught by testing

What it does NOT do

It has no effect at all unless require-trusted-types-for is also present — allowlisting policy names is meaningless if nothing requires policies to be used in the first place. It also does not restrict what a policy's rules can DO, only whether the policy can be CREATED under that name.

The single most common naming mix-up in this course require-trusted-types-for and trusted-types differ by exactly the words "require" and "for" — and by what they actually do. Read them side by side, slowly, at least once:
DirectiveJob
require-trusted-types-for 'script'Turns ON enforcement, page-wide
trusted-types <names>Restricts WHICH policy names may exist
